# Comprehensive Nutrient and Soil Management Guide

## Nitrogen (N) Deficiency
Nitrogen is crucial for vegetative growth and chlorophyll production.
**Symptoms:**
- General yellowing (chlorosis) of older, lower leaves first (nitrogen is mobile in the plant, so the plant moves it to new growth).
- Stunted, slow growth.
- Spindly stems and smaller leaves.
**Management:**
- Apply Nitrogen-based fertilizers: Urea, Ammonium Sulfate, or CAN (Calcium Ammonium Nitrate).
- Incorporate organic matter (compost, FYM).
- Use green manure crops (e.g., Sesbania, Sunn hemp) and biofertilizers (Azotobacter, Rhizobium for legumes).
- Use split applications of Urea rather than a single large dose to prevent leaching and volatilization.

## Phosphorus (P) Deficiency
Phosphorus is essential for root development, flowering, and energy transfer (ATP).
**Symptoms:**
- Dark, bluish-green foliage.
- Purplish or reddish discoloration on the tips and margins of older leaves (very prominent in maize and tomatoes).
- Poor root development and delayed maturity.
**Management:**
- Apply Phosphorus fertilizers: DAP (Di-Ammonium Phosphate), SSP (Single Super Phosphate), or TSP.
- Apply at the time of sowing (basal dose) because phosphorus is immobile in the soil.
- Use Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB) to increase availability in the soil.
- Adjust soil pH (P is most available between pH 6.0 and 7.0).

## Potassium (K) Deficiency
Potassium regulates stomatal opening (water use efficiency), disease resistance, and fruit quality.
**Symptoms:**
- Scorching, browning, or "burning" of the margins and tips of older leaves.
- Weak stalks leading to lodging (falling over).
- Small, shriveled grains or poorly colored, low-quality fruits.
**Management:**
- Apply MOP (Muriate of Potash) or SOP (Sulphate of Potash).
- Apply in split doses in sandy soils where leaching occurs.
- Ensure adequate moisture, as dry soils restrict potassium uptake.

## Zinc (Zn) Deficiency
A very common micronutrient deficiency, especially in intensive rice-wheat systems and alkaline soils.
**Symptoms:**
- "Khaira" disease in rice: Rusty brown spots on older leaves, stunted growth.
- In maize: "White bud" (new leaves emerge pale yellow or white).
- Interveinal chlorosis (yellowing between veins) on younger leaves.
**Management:**
- Soil application of Zinc Sulfate (usually 25 kg/ha) at sowing/transplanting.
- Foliar spray of 0.5% Zinc Sulfate + 0.25% slaked lime if symptoms appear in standing crops.
- Do not mix Zinc fertilizers with Phosphorus fertilizers, as they form insoluble compounds.

## Iron (Fe) Deficiency
Common in calcareous soils (high calcium/high pH) and waterlogged soils.
**Symptoms:**
- Interveinal chlorosis of the youngest leaves (veins remain green while the tissue between them turns yellow or white).
- In severe cases, the entire leaf turns white.
**Management:**
- Foliar application of 1% Ferrous Sulfate solution (often mixed with a small amount of citric acid to keep it soluble).
- Soil application is generally ineffective in alkaline soils; use Iron chelates (Fe-EDDHA) if soil application is necessary.
- Improve soil drainage.

## Soil Salinity and Alkalinity
**Symptoms of Salinity (High soluble salts):**
- White crust on the soil surface.
- Plants appear drought-stressed even when the soil is wet (osmotic stress).
- Leaf tip burn and stunting.
**Management:**
- Provide adequate drainage.
- Leach salts below the root zone with heavy, deep irrigation using good quality water.
- Grow salt-tolerant crops (e.g., barley, cotton, sugarbeet).

**Symptoms of Alkalinity (High sodium, pH > 8.5):**
- Poor soil structure (hard and crusty when dry, sticky when wet), poor water infiltration.
- Black powdery residue on the surface (dissolved organic matter).
**Management:**
- Apply soil amendments like Gypsum (Calcium Sulfate) or agricultural sulfur.
- Grow green manure crops like Dhaincha (Sesbania aculeata) and incorporate them into the soil.
- Ensure deep ploughing to break hardpans.

## Soil Acidification (Low pH < 5.5)
**Symptoms:**
- Poor plant growth due to aluminum or manganese toxicity.
- Deficiencies in Calcium, Magnesium, and Phosphorus (which become locked up).
**Management:**
- Apply agricultural lime (Calcium Carbonate) or Dolomite (contains Calcium and Magnesium).
- Use acid-tolerant crops if liming is not feasible.
