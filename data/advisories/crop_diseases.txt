# Comprehensive Crop Disease Management Guide

## Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)
Late blight is a devastating disease affecting potatoes and tomatoes.
**Symptoms:** 
- Small, light to dark green, circular to irregular-shaped water-soaked spots on leaves.
- Lesions expand rapidly into large, dark brown or black necrotic areas.
- White fungal growth may appear on the undersides of leaves during high humidity.
- Potato tubers show reddish-brown, dry, granular rot.
- Tomato fruits develop dark, greasy-looking lesions.
**Favorable Conditions:** High humidity (above 90%), cool nights (10-15°C), and warm days (15-20°C).
**Management:** 
- Use resistant varieties if available.
- Practice crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops (e.g., cereals) for at least 3 years.
- Destroy cull piles and volunteer potato plants.
- Apply preventative fungicides like Mancozeb or Chlorothalonil before the disease appears.
- If symptoms are present, use systemic fungicides like Metalaxyl + Mancozeb, Cymoxanil, or Dimethomorph.

## Rust Diseases (Puccinia spp.)
Rusts affect a wide range of crops including wheat (stem, leaf, stripe rust), maize, soybean, and beans.
**Symptoms:**
- Small, raised, orange, rust-colored, or brown pustules on leaves, stems, and sheaths.
- Heavily infected leaves may yellow and die prematurely.
- Reduces grain fill and causes lodging.
**Favorable Conditions:** High humidity, frequent dews, and moderate temperatures (15-25°C).
**Management:**
- Grow resistant or tolerant varieties (the most effective method).
- Adjust planting dates to avoid peak infection periods.
- Eliminate volunteer hosts and alternative hosts.
- Apply foliar fungicides like Tebuconazole, Propiconazole, or Azoxystrobin at early onset.

## Powdery Mildew
Affects wheat, barley, grapes, cucurbits, and apples.
**Symptoms:**
- White to light gray powdery patches on the upper surfaces of leaves, stems, and sometimes fruit.
- Leaves may yellow, curl, and die prematurely.
**Favorable Conditions:** High humidity but no free water on leaves, moderate temperatures (15-22°C), dense canopies.
**Management:**
- Improve air circulation through proper spacing and pruning.
- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization which promotes lush growth.
- Use sulfur-based fungicides (avoid in high heat).
- Apply systemic fungicides like Triazoles (e.g., Hexaconazole, Myclobutanil) or Strobilurins.

## Downy Mildew
Affects grapes, cucurbits, onions, and sunflower.
**Symptoms:**
- Pale green to yellow angular spots on the upper leaf surface, bounded by leaf veins.
- Gray to purplish fuzzy growth on the underside of the leaf corresponding to the spots.
**Favorable Conditions:** Cool temperatures (10-20°C) and prolonged periods of leaf wetness.
**Management:**
- Ensure good drainage and air circulation.
- Water at the base of plants rather than overhead.
- Apply protectant fungicides like Copper oxychloride or Mancozeb.
- Use systemic fungicides like Metalaxyl or Fosetyl-Al for curative action.

## Rice Blast (Magnaporthe oryzae)
The most significant disease of rice worldwide.
**Symptoms:**
- Spindle-shaped or eye-shaped lesions with grayish centers and brown margins on leaves.
- "Neck blast" occurs when the node just below the panicle is infected, causing the panicle to fall over and resulting in "whiteheads" or empty grains.
**Favorable Conditions:** Extended periods of high humidity, frequent rain, and temperatures between 25-28°C.
**Management:**
- Use resistant varieties.
- Avoid excessive and split application of nitrogen.
- Manage water carefully (avoid prolonged drainage).
- Apply fungicides such as Tricyclazole, Isoprothiolane, or Kasugamycin at the booting or heading stage.

## Bacterial Leaf Blight of Rice (Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae)
**Symptoms:**
- Water-soaked streaks on leaf margins that enlarge and turn yellowish-white with wavy edges.
- Ooze may be visible on lesions early in the morning.
- Can cause "Kresek" (seedling wilt) in young plants.
**Favorable Conditions:** Typhoons, heavy rain, strong winds, and high nitrogen levels.
**Management:**
- Use resistant varieties.
- Avoid excessive nitrogen.
- Drain the field temporarily to reduce humidity.
- Chemical control is generally ineffective, but copper compounds may provide some suppression.

## Smut Diseases
Affects cereals like wheat, barley, maize, and sugarcane.
**Symptoms:**
- Floral parts or grains are replaced by masses of black, powdery fungal spores.
- Often visible only at the heading stage.
**Management:**
- Seed treatment with systemic fungicides (e.g., Carboxin, Tebuconazole) is the most effective control method.
- Use certified disease-free seed.
- Crop rotation.

## Mosaic Viruses (e.g., Tobacco Mosaic, Cucumber Mosaic, Papaya Ringspot)
**Symptoms:**
- Mottling, yellowing, or mosaic patterns on leaves.
- Stunting, leaf distortion (curling, wrinkling).
- Reduced fruit yield and quality (often malformed or discolored fruit).
**Favorable Conditions:** Presence of insect vectors (aphids, whiteflies, thrips) or mechanical transmission (tools, hands).
**Management:**
- Plant resistant varieties.
- Control insect vectors using insecticides or reflective mulches.
- Remove and destroy infected plants (roguing) immediately.
- Disinfect tools and wash hands frequently.
- Control weed hosts around the field.
