# Comprehensive Environmental Stress and Weed Management Guide

## Drought Stress Management
Drought stress severely limits crop yields due to insufficient soil moisture.
**Symptoms:**
- Wilting of leaves, starting during the hottest part of the day and progressing to permanent wilting.
- Stunted growth, delayed flowering, and reduced grain filling.
- Leaf curling or rolling (common in maize and rice).
**Management:**
- Select short-duration and drought-tolerant crop varieties.
- Practice conservation agriculture: zero tillage and crop residue retention (mulching) to conserve soil moisture.
- Implement Micro-Irrigation Schemes (PMKSY) such as drip or sprinkler irrigation to maximize water use efficiency.
- Apply Potassium fertilizers, which improve stomatal regulation and water retention in plants.
- Spray 1-2% Potassium Nitrate (KNO3) during dry spells.
- Use anti-transpirants (e.g., Kaolin spray) to reduce water loss from leaves.

## Waterlogging and Flood Stress
Excess water restricts oxygen flow to the roots (hypoxia/anoxia).
**Symptoms:**
- Yellowing (chlorosis) of lower leaves due to nitrogen deficiency (denitrification occurs rapidly in waterlogged soils).
- Stunted growth and wilting (despite abundant water, roots cannot function).
- Root rot and foul-smelling soil.
**Management:**
- Construct proper surface drainage channels (e.g., ridge and furrow system, raised beds).
- Grow flood-tolerant varieties (e.g., Swarna-Sub1 for rice, which can survive submergence for up to 14 days).
- Delay top-dressing of fertilizers until water drains.
- Apply a foliar spray of 1-2% Urea or Potassium Nitrate after the water recedes to help the crop recover quickly.

## Frost and Cold Stress
Occurs when temperatures drop near or below freezing.
**Symptoms:**
- Water-soaked, dark green, or blackened leaves (tissue death due to ice crystals rupturing cells).
- Shriveling of grains if frost hits during the dough stage (e.g., in wheat).
- Flower drop and fruit damage in horticultural crops.
**Management:**
- Apply light irrigation before a predicted frost night (wet soil retains and releases more heat than dry soil).
- Create smoke screens around the field borders during the early morning hours to trap outgoing long-wave radiation.
- Avoid late planting of winter crops.
- Spray 0.1% Thiourea to provide some frost tolerance.

## Heat Stress
Occurs during terminal growth stages, particularly threatening wheat yields in South Asia ("terminal heat").
**Symptoms:**
- Leaf scorching and sunburn on fruits.
- Forced maturity: grains dry out prematurely, resulting in shriveled, lightweight grains.
- Poor pollination and flower drop.
**Management:**
- Adjust planting dates (e.g., early sowing of wheat) to ensure the grain-filling stage occurs before peak temperatures.
- Maintain adequate soil moisture through frequent, light irrigations.
- Apply foliar sprays of Potassium or Salicylic acid to mitigate heat shock.

## Integrated Weed Management
Weeds compete with crops for water, nutrients, sunlight, and space.
**Critical Period:** The first 30-45 days after sowing are usually the critical period for weed competition.
**Management Strategies:**
- **Cultural:** Crop rotation, intercropping, appropriate plant spacing, and use of cover crops.
- **Mechanical/Physical:** Hand weeding, hoeing, and using tractor-drawn cultivators or weeders. Stale seedbed technique (irrigate to flush out weeds, kill them, then plant).
- **Chemical (Herbicides):** 
  - *Pre-emergence:* Applied before crops and weeds emerge (e.g., Pendimethalin).
  - *Post-emergence:* Applied after weeds emerge (e.g., Bispyribac-sodium for rice, 2,4-D for broadleaf weeds in wheat).
  - Always follow recommended doses and safety guidelines to avoid phytotoxicity and herbicide resistance.
